1How to read a figure in this document

Every number carries a grade for where it came from. This is not decoration. A national statistical office and a crowd-sourced salary aggregator are not the same kind of fact, and nothing here treats them as one.

A A Official statistics or a regulator. Eurostat, OECD, a national statistics office, a ministry, a met service. Treat as fact.
B B Primary legal or institutional text, or a peer-reviewed paper. Accurate, but read the scope conditions.
C C Reported by a named outlet, a law firm, or an industry body. Usually right, occasionally stale.
D D Salary aggregator or self-reported crowd data. Indicative only. Aggregators disagree by two to three times for the same city.

A grade describes the source, not the certainty of the conclusion. A grade A rent index for a whole city still tells you little about the specific flat you will be shown. Where a figure is a planning band rather than a measurement, it says so.

Why the scores are what they are

Each score from 0 to 10 is a judgement, but it is anchored where an anchor exists. Savings is driven by the Eurostat net-to-gross ratio for the country and the rent-to-net ratio for the city, both in section 6. Safety is driven by Eurostat's age-standardised self-harm rate and its crime-perception series, both in section 8. Both markets and Anna's entry are the two that rest on judgement rather than measurement, and they carry 40 per cent of the default weight between them, which is the honest weak point of this model. Where a score would move a city more than two places, the reason is stated in full rather than summarised.

What the arithmetic actually says. In 25 of the 33 cities modelled, Sebi's net income alone covers two people, so the EUR 35,000 is never touched and Anna can stay unpaid for as long as she needs. The savings are not the constraint. In the eight where his income does not cover both of you, the monthly shortfall is under EUR 300 in five of them, which savings absorb for years. Only Florence, Milan and Rome produce a shortfall large enough for the runway to be measured in months rather than years, and all three fail on Sebi's pay floor anyway.

So the real day-one number is the upfront cost, not the burn rate. That is where EUR 35,000 genuinely bites. Copenhagen takes 29 per cent of your savings before you sleep there a single night, because Danish law permits three months deposit plus three months prepaid rent. Aarhus takes 20 per cent, Geneva and Zurich 18 per cent each, Munich 15. Against that, Paris takes 4 per cent, because the deposit is capped at one month and Visale replaces the guarantor for free. The honest ranking on money is by what leaves your account in week one.

And the thing savings actually buy. Not months of survival, in most of these cities, but the freedom for Anna to refuse the first bad offer. That is worth more in a thick market than a thin one, which is the argument for Utrecht, Rotterdam and Amsterdam over Bucharest and Porto, where the money would last far longer and buy nothing.

5The framework, and why each weight

Your original list scored climate, work, safety, environment and language. It was sound but it optimised for the first year. What follows is the argument for each weight in the model above, with the evidence it actually rests on rather than the evidence it would be convenient to have.

5.1 The urban premium is real but small, and that changes the advice

The familiar claim is that urban workers earn about a third more, and Butts, Jaworski and Kitchens are often cited for it. Their paper says close to the opposite. Their whole contribution is to show that the naive gap is mostly sorting: more capable people move to cities, and a raw comparison credits the city with what the person brought. Once that is corrected, the United States urban wage premium runs at about 12 per cent in 1940 and 1950 and falls to a few per cent by 2010. So "move to a big city and earn a third more" is not a finding, it is the error the paper was written to correct.

Two things do survive, and they are the ones the model uses.

Big-city experience is portable. Eliasson and Westerlund, working on Swedish population registers, find dynamic agglomeration effects among graduates that persist after the person relocates to a smaller city, and that are stronger for higher-ability workers. This is the real argument for starting somewhere thick: not that the salary is a third higher, but that the learning stays with you when you leave.

The escalator exists but has weakened. Fielding's original finding, that the South East of England attracts upwardly mobile young adults, promotes them faster and lets them step off later, is a genuine result. Champion's 2021 replication finds London's escalator is weaker than it was, so the idea holds in outline but not at its original strength.

Butts, Jaworski and Kitchens, The Urban Wage Premium in Historical Perspective, NBER WP 31387, published in Regional Science and Urban Economics 2026 B. Eliasson and Westerlund, The urban wage premium and spatial sorting on observed and unobserved ability, Journal of Economic Geography 23(3), 2023, 601 to 627 B. Fielding, Regional Studies 26(1), 1992 and Champion, Population, Space and Place, 2021 B.

What this means for you. A thick ML metro still compounds for Sebi, but through skill rather than through a large pay gap, and the effect is modest. That weakens the case for tolerating a bad city on the grounds that it is big. It does not weaken the case for Anna being inside a real ecosystem early, which rests on the next argument, not this one.

5.2 Treat the two careers as one constraint. This is the strongest thing in the document

Your list scored Sebi's salary and Anna's employability as separate rows. The economics says score them jointly, and the literature here is much better than the urban-premium literature.

Mincer's model shows family ties act as a negative externality on migration: they deter moves, and where a move happens they depress the earnings of the partner who did not drive it. That partner is the tied mover. Costa and Kahn document the consequence at scale: the share of college-educated couples in large metros rose from 32 per cent in 1940 to 50 per cent in 1990, and they argue this is precisely because a large labour market is the only kind that solves two careers at once.

Venator's 2026 work adds the piece that matters most for you. Occupations have become more geographically concentrated since the 1980s, couples increasingly choose occupations that cluster in the same labour markets, and being well matched to your partner on occupational clustering is positively associated with earnings for the secondary earner specifically.

Mincer, Family Migration Decisions, Journal of Political Economy 86(5), 1978, 749 to 773 B. Costa and Kahn, Power Couples, Quarterly Journal of Economics 115(4), 2000, 1287 to 1315 B. Venator, Concentrating on his career or hers?, Labour Economics 99, 2026 B.

Why Anna is structurally the tied mover here, and it is not about either of you. ESG, impact assessment and planning consulting is both more geographically concentrated and more language-gated than ML engineering. ML work is English-workable in every city on this list. Her field is workable in her existing languages in roughly six of them. That asymmetry is a property of the two occupations, not of the two people.

The rule that follows. A city passes only if both markets are genuinely present, or if Sebi's income alone funds Anna's runway into an ecosystem that actually exists there. Applying just this one rule drops Geneva and Rome, both of which pay well and offer her nothing but an unpaid internship, and lifts the Netherlands.

5.3 The ground under Anna's field moved in March

ESG demand is not a given. A large share of junior ESG and sustainability-reporting hiring in Europe exists because the Corporate Sustainability Reporting Directive obliged companies to produce audited sustainability statements. The Omnibus I directive, adopted by the Council on 24 February 2026 and in force from 18 March 2026, cuts that scope to companies with more than 1,000 employees and EUR 450 million turnover. Parliament had pushed for a 1,750-employee threshold; the Council's tighter number prevailed.

That removes a large tranche of mid-sized companies from the obligation. It does not abolish the field: the largest reporters remain in scope, sustainable finance disclosure is a separate regime, and energy-transition and impact-assessment work has its own drivers. But it is the single biggest downside risk to a plan whose whole logic is "buy Anna twelve months to enter a growing market", and it argues for weighting diversity of demand in a city, not just volume. Amsterdam, Brussels and Copenhagen have non-CSRD demand from funds, institutions and the energy transition. A market that was purely reporting-driven is the fragile kind.

7Climate and extreme heat

Fewer grey days counts in favour. Cold does not count against. The heat rule in the model is "no regular heat above 42.5 °C", measured against all-time station records, and those two things are not the same at all.

The rule measured the wrong thing. An all-time record is a single afternoon, sometimes forty years ago, at one station. It tells you almost nothing about whether a place is habitable in summer. Two examples from this list make the point: Bucharest clears the rule on a 42.2 °C record and still has hot, dry continental summers, and Milan, which has genuinely oppressive humid summers with tropical nights, passes comfortably because its dry-bulb record is unremarkable.

What the literature uses instead. Copernicus reports heat as days of "strong" or greater heat stress, on a felt-temperature index that combines temperature, humidity, wind and radiation. In 2025, 41 per cent of Europe had more such days than average, and southern and eastern Spain recorded up to 50 more days than average above a 32 °C felt temperature. That is the metric that corresponds to what you would actually experience, and by it Barcelona looks worse and Milan looks much worse than the record-high column suggests.

How it is handled here. The 42.5 °C rule stays, because it is yours, every record it depends on is sourced to a station, and it is a switch in the model rather than a hard-coded assumption. It removes exactly one city, Florence, and that is not the city you would remove if you were choosing on summer comfort.

Sebi: Spanish citizenship against the Romanian passport. The advice has not changed and it is the most important paragraph in this document.

Spain's exemption from renouncing a prior nationality covers Ibero-American countries, Andorra, the Philippines, Equatorial Guinea and Portugal, plus France by treaty. Romania is not on that list, so under Article 23 of the Civil Code a renunciation declaration would be required at the oath. Spanish administrative doctrine has treated this as a declaration whose effect on the other nationality is not Spain's concern, and Romania permits multiple citizenship under Law 21/1991, so in practice the Romanian passport is not lost unless he separately files to renounce with Romanian authorities. That is practice and doctrine, not statute, and it is exactly the kind of thing that should not be relied on from a document like this one.

A Spain and Romania dual-nationality working group was agreed at the November 2022 summit, and in February 2025 Spain's foreign minister said drafts had been exchanged and a definitive text was being worked on. As of September 2026 it remains unsigned. If signed it would remove the problem entirely for roughly 600,000 Romanians in Spain.

Recommendation, unchanged: take no step that risks the Romanian passport, because it is the legal basis of this entire move. Get written advice from a Spanish immigration lawyer, and if the treaty is genuinely close, waiting costs little given that the ten-year clock has not started.

Anna: the Swiss grandmother. Probably closed, and worth confirming now rather than later.

Since the Federal Act on Swiss Citizenship took effect on 1 January 2018, citizenship no longer passes to grandchildren, and the earlier simplified route for descendants of a Swiss grandmother closed on 31 December 2017. Swiss nationality by descent now runs parent to child. Having Swiss grandparents alone is not sufficient. The one remaining path is through Anna's parent: if that parent holds Swiss citizenship, or can first reclaim it, she could then claim through them. Worth one enquiry to a Swiss consulate. Get professional advice on these two. Do not act on this document alone.

Data gaps we did not paper over.

Anna's pay is the weakest data in this document and always was. Almost every figure for her field is grade D, drawn from aggregators that disagree by wide margins, because there is no equivalent of Levels.fyi for sustainability consulting. The Eurostat sector means give a sanity check for ICT and nothing comparable for her. Treat her salary column as an order of magnitude, not a number.

Sebi's ML salary for the Basque Country, Trondheim and Andorra was not researched, and Vienna's entry pay for Anna is a German proxy rather than a sourced Austrian figure.

City rents are asking prices from portals, not transaction data, and asking prices run above achieved rents in slow markets and below them in fast ones. The Dutch, Norwegian, Finnish and Luxembourg figures come from official or portal-official sources and are the most reliable; the Belgian, Italian and Portuguese ones are the least.

Net take-home above the average wage is a planning estimate throughout. The Eurostat anchors in section 6 are measured at each country's average wage, and Sebi would earn above it almost everywhere.

We did not model: employer pension contributions, the Italian inbound workers regime, the Danish researcher scheme, or Portugal's IFICI regime, any of which could move a city by a lot for a specific offer.

What would actually change the ranking

A model you cannot falsify is not worth much. These are the specific findings that would reorder the top of the table, in rough order of how likely they are:

  • A real offer for Anna anywhere. This dominates everything else in this document. A funded role in her field in any surviving city should override the model outright. Re-run this the moment either of you has one.
  • Sebi securing a remote or foreign-payer contract. Tick the scenario box in section 2 and watch Barcelona go to the top. This is the single biggest lever either of you controls, and it converts the best social and language fit on the list from third place to first.
  • The Omnibus scope cut biting harder than expected on junior ESG hiring. If mid-sized-company demand falls away through 2026 and 2027, cities whose ESG market is reporting-driven suffer and cities with fund, institutional and energy-transition demand hold up. That favours Amsterdam, Brussels and Copenhagen over Berlin and Milan.
  • The Spain and Romania treaty being signed. It removes the only real obstacle to Sebi taking Spanish citizenship, which makes the ten-year Spanish clock worth starting and strengthens the Barcelona and Madrid case considerably.
  • Dutch rent policy. The shortage is falling for the second consecutive year. If that continues, the one serious cost of the Dutch plan shrinks. If the free-sector sell-off reported by Pararius accelerates instead, it grows.
  • Anything that makes you weight Anna's market at 30 per cent rather than 15. Tick the third scenario box. Brussels and Amsterdam rise, Berlin and Munich fall, and the answer stops being a compromise.

The decision, stated plainly

If you optimise for both careers plus liveability, the Netherlands is still the strongest single answer, but the city inside it has changed. Utrecht or Rotterdam, not Amsterdam. Same national ESG market, same Dutch pay, roughly EUR 450 a month less rent, and a far less punishing flat hunt. The Netherlands has the worst market-rent overburden rate in western Europe at 39.5 per cent, and the whole point of choosing a smaller Randstad city is that you keep the market and drop most of that cost. Start the flat search before the job search either way.

If you optimise for Anna's fit, fast social integration and climate, Barcelona, and now with two named obstacles rather than one. Her native languages unlock the field, the ESG scene is large and people are open. Sebi's pay does not clear the floor at junior or mid level, and rent control has cut long-term supply by about 9 per cent while closing the seasonal and room-rental fallbacks. A remote or foreign-payer ML contract removes the first constraint entirely and would make Barcelona the outright winner. The second constraint is a queue, and queues are beaten with time and local contacts, which she has.

If Sebi's trajectory leads, Berlin, and the case for it is stronger than it was. Deepest ML ecosystem, the best measured rent-to-income position of any strong market here at 13.2 per cent overburden, genuinely open socially in a way the rest of Germany is not, no heat risk, five years to a passport with dual nationality allowed. The honest cost is that Anna waits 6 to 15 months in English while learning German, which is the longest wait of the three.

Andorra and Liechtenstein, both blocked on right to work.

Before choosing between three cities, it is worth spending a month finding out whether that is achievable, because it is worth more than the difference between any two rows in this table.
