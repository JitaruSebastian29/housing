2The framework, rebuilt

Your original list scored climate, work, safety, environment and language. It was sound but it optimised for the first year. Five things were missing.

2.1 Why this choice compounds

  • The urban wage premium is large and, importantly, partly portable. The average urban worker earns roughly a third more than non-urban counterparts, a pattern that holds across countries and across the last eighty years. Swedish register data finds the premium from big-city work experience is not lost when someone later moves to a smaller city, which means it reflects learning and human capital rather than just local prices, and the effect is stronger for higher-ability workers.
    Butts and Jaworski, The Urban Wage Premium in Historical Perspective. Eliasson and Westerlund, Journal of Economic Geography 23(3), 2023.
  • Fielding's escalator region idea, now well replicated: large metros attract upwardly mobile young adults, promote them faster, and let them step off later while keeping the gains. Entering the labour market in an urban area is associated with higher cumulative lifetime income, with the gap widening later in life.
    Urban Studies Research, regional escalators. Longitudinal and Life Course Studies 13(3), 2022.
  • What this means for you specifically: a thick ML metro compounds faster for Sebi than a thin one at similar starting pay, and for Anna, being inside the right professional ecosystem early is itself the investment. That is the argument for her taking an internship, and it is a good argument.

2.2 Treat the two careers as one constraint

This is the biggest change. Your list scored Sebi's salary and Anna's employability as separate rows. The economics says score them jointly. Mincer's dual-earner model, and the empirical work since, shows two sets of location preferences produce lower mobility and a higher chance that one partner ends up somewhere individually sub-optimal for their career. That partner is the tied mover. The friction is worst in occupations concentrated in a few regions.

ESG, impact assessment and planning consulting is more geographically concentrated and more language-gated than ML engineering. So Anna is the structural tied mover here, and her market has to be the primary filter rather than a secondary score.

Occupational co-agglomeration in dual-earner households. Overview: dual-career couples.

Rule to adopt. A city passes only if both markets are genuinely present, or if Sebi's income alone funds Anna's runway into an ecosystem that actually exists there. Applying just this one rule drops Geneva and lifts the Netherlands.

2.3 Compare net savings, not gross salary

Rent should be a share of take-home pay. The OECD tax wedge for a single worker on the average wage was 35.1% of labour cost across the OECD in 2025, the highest since 2016, with the largest wedges in Belgium at 52.5%, Germany 49.3%, France 47.2%, Austria 47.1% and Italy 45.8%. Two costs sit outside that figure and matter here: Dutch mandatory basic health insurance at roughly EUR 140 to 160 per person per month, and Swiss mandatory health insurance at roughly CHF 300 to 450 per person per month. Both come out after tax.

2.5 Citizenship, where the evidence stops and a lawyer starts

Sebi: Spanish citizenship against the Romanian passport

  • Spain's exemption from renouncing a prior nationality covers Ibero-American countries, Andorra, the Philippines, Equatorial Guinea and Portugal, plus France by a 2021 treaty. Romania is not on that list, so under Article 23 of the Civil Code the renunciation declaration would be required at the oath.
  • In practice Spanish administrative doctrine treats this as a declaration only, and has held that it is irrelevant whether the renunciation actually causes loss of the other nationality. Romania permits multiple citizenship, so the Romanian passport is not lost unless Sebi separately files to renounce with Romanian authorities.
  • A Spain and Romania dual-nationality working group was agreed at the November 2022 summit, and in February 2025 Spain's foreign minister said drafts had been exchanged and a definitive text was being worked on. It remains unsigned. If signed it removes the problem entirely and would cover roughly 600,000 Romanians in Spain.
  • Recommendation: do not take any step that risks the Romanian passport, since it is the legal basis of this entire move. Get written advice from a Spanish immigration lawyer, and if the treaty is close, consider waiting.
Echeverría Abogados on DGRN doctrine. Legal Fournier on Articles 23 and 24. Treaty status via The Local.

Anna: the Swiss grandmother

Probably closed, and worth knowing now rather than later. Since the Federal Act on Swiss Citizenship took effect on 1 January 2018, citizenship no longer passes to grandchildren, and the earlier simplified route for descendants of a Swiss grandmother closed on 31 December 2017. Swiss nationality by descent now runs parent to child. Having Swiss grandparents alone is not sufficient. The one remaining path is through Anna's parent: if that parent holds Swiss citizenship, or can first reclaim it, Anna could then claim through them. Worth a single enquiry to a Swiss consulate, but do not build the plan on it.

The decision, stated plainly

If you optimise for both careers plus liveability, the Netherlands is the strongest single answer. It is the only place where Sebi clears his floor with margin, helped by the 30% ruling, and Anna can enter a thick English-workable ESG market within months. Accept that Sebi's pay is tight and the rent-controlled market is hard to crack. A remote or foreign-payer ML contract removes that constraint entirely and would make Barcelona the outright winner.

If Sebi's trajectory leads, Berlin.
